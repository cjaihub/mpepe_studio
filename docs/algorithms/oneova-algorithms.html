1. Principles

  • Satisfaction, not raw time. We optimize for watch time that people are glad they spent: completions, rewatches, follows and shares. Quick skips, "not interested" taps and hides count against a video. YouTube moved to this after watch-time-only ranking rewarded clickbait.
  • The interest graph comes first and the social graph second. As on TikTok, anyone's video can reach anyone. As on YouTube Shorts, the people you follow and your watch history give the first strong signals.
  • Every video gets a fair first audience. Staged audience pools (section 2.4) let new creators break out on merit.
  • Explainable. Every score keeps its breakdown, so "Why am I seeing this" answers from real numbers. ViralScoreBreakdown already does this.
  • Habit-forming, never manipulative. We want people to come back because the feed is good. We do not trap them: users get break reminders, a daily-limit setting and quiet hours, and we never send fake notifications. This also keeps us inside Google Play's policies and app-store reviews.
  • Real data only. No seeded or fake content and no inflated counters. All counters are server-authoritative.

2. Feed engine

The feed engine is a four-stage funnel, run per request (one page is 10–20 videos), with prefetch of the next page while the user watches. Existing code: RecommendationService (lib/backend/social/services/recommendation_service.dart), oneova_neural_ranker.dart, online_feature_store.dart, viral_velocity_score.dart. PARTIAL

candidates (≈600) ─▶ filter (≈400) ─▶ rank (score) ─▶ re-rank (diversity/explore/ads) ─▶ page (10–20)

2.1 Candidate sources (blended)

SourceShareIdea taken from

Following: new posts from creators you follow and interact with15%YouTube subscriptions

2.2 Filters

Remove: already watched in the last 7 days (unless rewatched), blocked or hidden creators, hidden topics, "not interested" items, policy-flagged content, followers-only posts from creators you do not follow, and anything outside the user's age and region rules. BUILT in _applyPolicyFiltering / _applyFeedbackSignals.

2.3 Ranking score

Each candidate gets predicted probabilities from the ranker, with features from the online feature store. The final score is a weighted sum of expected value minus expected regret:

score = 1.0·P(complete) + 0.8·P(rewatch) + 0.6·E[watch fraction]
      + 0.5·P(like) + 1.2·P(comment) + 1.5·P(share) + 1.2·P(save) + 2.0·P(follow)
      − 1.5·P(skip < 2s) − 3.0·P(not interested) − 5.0·P(report)
      × creatorQuality(0.7–1.15) × freshness(age) × localeMatch(0.6–1.0)
  • Watch fraction is capped at 1.0 for a single view, and rewatches count separately. Short videos should not win just because they are short. This is the main lesson from YouTube Shorts.
  • Completion and rewatch carry the most weight, as on TikTok. They are the strongest signal that a video is good.
  • freshness(age) = 1.0 for the first 48 h, then falls to 0.6 by day 30. Evergreen videos can still rank on their probabilities.
  • Until the neural ranker is trained, the cold-start fallback is the viral-velocity score: score = (ln(1 + points/hour) × quality + freshness) × decay, already in code.

2.4 Staged audience pools (how videos go viral)

Every new video goes through pools. It is promoted when its engagement in the pool beats the creator's niche baseline:

PoolAudiencePromote when (vs. niche median)
P0 Seed~300 views: followers plus interest matchescompletion ≥ 1.1× and skip rate ≤ 0.9×
P1 Test~3,000completion ≥ 1.2×, shares + saves per view ≥ 1.2×
P2 Grow~30,000the same, sustained for 6 h; no rise in reports
P3 Broad100k+, cross-regionvelocity still rising (section 4); human spot-check above 1M

A video that stalls stays in its pool and keeps getting views from strong interest matches. It can be promoted again later if engagement improves (a "second wave"). Implemented as a server job that writes content/{id}.pool. TO BUILD

2.5 Re-ranking

  • No more than 1 video per creator in any 5 consecutive slots, and no more than 2 per topic in a row.
  • An exploration slot about every 6th position.
  • Ad slots are placed by section 5. They never interrupt the first 3 videos of a session.
  • Live now: when someone you follow is live, their live card can appear once per session in slots 2–5.

2.6 Learning loop

Events (section 9) stream to user_events. User interest vectors update in near real time: an exponential moving average over the last 200 completions, with half-life ≈ 3 days. Topic affinities decay by 2% per day. The ranker is retrained nightly from BigQuery exports; scheduledFeedbackBigQueryExport already exists.

3. Looping and session flow

  • Seamless loop: short videos (≤ 60 s) loop by default, with no end card. Each loop counts as a rewatch signal, but only up to 3 per view so a forgotten phone does not inflate the count.
  • Preload: keep the next 2 videos buffered and the previous 1 warm. Start playback at the first frame within 150 ms, so swiping never shows a spinner.
  • Session memory: leaving the app and coming back within 30 minutes resumes the same feed position.
  • Wellbeing: an optional "take a break" card after the user's chosen limit, plus a daily time setting. Both are off by default and easy to find in Feed controls.

4. Trending algorithm

PARTIAL viral_trending_engine.dart, viral_velocity_score.dart. Trending is about acceleration against a baseline, not total views, so a small niche can trend:

velocity_t   = weighted engagement points in the last hour
trendScore   = (velocity_t − μ_topic,region) / σ_topic,region      (z-score vs. 14-day baseline)
             × acceleration (velocity_t / velocity_t−1, capped 3.0)
             × uniqueness (distinct engagers / total engagements)
trending if trendScore ≥ 3 and ≥ 50 distinct engagers in the last hour
  • The same formula runs for videos, hashtags, sounds and live streams, per region and language, every 10 minutes.
  • Anti-manipulation: engagement from accounts younger than 24 h, from one device cluster, or in bursts with identical timing counts 0.1×. The uniqueness factor defeats like-farms.
  • Trending surfaces: the Discover tab, the trending sounds picker in the camera, and 10% of feed candidates.

5. Advertising algorithm

Goal: the app should feel free and uncluttered while ads fund it. The rule is that an ad has to earn its slot against the organic video it replaces. TO BUILD

5.1 Ad load (how often)

baseGap  = 8 organic videos between ads (new users: 12 for their first 7 days)
gap      = baseGap × fatigue × tenureFactor
fatigue  = 1 + 0.25 × (ads skipped < 2s in last 10)        → more skips, fewer ads
caps     : ≥ 60 s between ads · ≤ 6 ads per hour · never in first 3 videos
           never during a live, a call or the first 30 s after an O-gesture to Ova

5.2 Which ad (auction)

adRank  = bid × P(click or conversion) × adQuality × userRelevance
show the ad only if  adRank × valueToUser ≥ score of the organic video it displaces × λ
                     (λ = 0.6 at launch; tune with revenue vs. retention tests)
price   = second-price: next adRank / (winner's P × quality) + 0.01
  • Formats: full-screen native video ads that look like posts and are labeled Sponsored. They are skippable at once and loop like normal videos. The same formats serve Biz products ("Shop" chip) and sponsored lives.
  • Frequency capping: the same creative at most twice a day per user, and the same advertiser at most 4 times.
  • Rewarded ads (opt-in): watching a 15–30 s ad gives AI Lab credits. This makes premium AI feel free and earns more per view than in-feed ads.
  • Creator share: 50% of the ad revenue earned next to a creator's videos goes to that creator, which motivates them to post more.
  • Privacy: targeting uses on-platform interests only. No sensitive categories (health, religion, politics, sexuality) and no third-party tracking. This keeps the Play "Data safety" declaration simple.

6. Referral program (5%)

When you invite someone, you earn 5% of what they earn on Oneova (creator ad share, live gifts, Biz sales payouts) for 12 months after they join. The platform pays the 5% from its own share; it is never deducted from the invitee. Single level only: no earnings from your invitee's invitees. This keeps the program clear of pyramid-scheme rules and store policy. TO BUILD

on invitee payout event (amount A, type ∈ {adShare, gifts, bizPayout}):
    if referral.active and now < referral.joinedAt + 365d and invitee.qualified:
        reward = round(A × 0.05, 2)
        ledger.add(referrer, reward, source = invitee, event)   ← Cloud Function, transaction
qualified = invitee verified phone + 7 days active + 1 post or 1 purchase
  • Links: oneova.app/i/{code}. Install attribution goes through Play Install Referrer, and the code is stored in referrals/{inviteeUid} at sign-up.
  • Anti-fraud: no self-referral (same device or payment method). At most 50 qualified invitees a month without review. Rewards stay pending for 14 days so refunds and chargebacks can be reversed. Payouts above a threshold need KYC.
  • Invitee welcome: new users get a starter credit pack for AI Lab, so joining through an invite feels like a gift.
  • All money movement is server-side only. Clients never write ledger documents (Firestore rules: deny writes).

7. Ova: engagement without annoyance

Shipped (this session): drawing an "O" is the only touch gesture that opens Ova. Double tap, double- and triple-tap holds, and the draw-"1" stroke were deleted from OvaGestureDetector, and tests check that they no longer open Ova. BUILT

7.1 Live suggestion strip (Ella-style)

When Ova opens, a small floating panel shows 3–5 action chips that scroll upward like TikTok's floating comments, e.g. "Reply to Amina", "Go live (you usually do at 8 pm)", "Continue the edit you left". Suggestions are not hard-coded: they are ranked from the user's own signals. TO BUILD

candidates = screen-context actions (what is on screen / circled)
           ∪ habit actions (frequent sequences: screen A → action B, by hour/day)
           ∪ pending items (unread messages, call-backs, drafts, notifications)
rank       = P(tap | context) × recency × (1 − recentlyDismissed)
show top 5, refresh as context changes; never repeat a dismissed chip for 24 h
  • Habit model: on-device n-gram counts over navigation events (route → next route/action) with time-of-day buckets. Cheap, private and quick to learn.
  • Not annoying: Ova never pops up by itself. Suggestions appear only after the user draws O. At most 1 proactive notification a day, opt-in, with quiet hours respected. Each chip can be dismissed with one swipe.

8. Notification memory, style learning and voice cloning

Consent and disclosure are requirements, not options. These features read private data and can speak as the user. Google Play allows them only with a prominent in-app disclosure, explicit opt-in, and a clear purpose. Impersonation must never be hidden from the people receiving it.

8.1 Notification memory TO BUILD

  • Android NotificationListenerService, enabled only by the user in system settings after an in-app explanation. It is a per-app allow list (e.g. WhatsApp yes, banking no) and is off by default for sensitive categories.
  • Stores sender, app, time, a text summary and the category in users/{uid}/notif_memory, encrypted, with 30-day retention by default. The user can view, search and delete everything.
  • Ova can answer "what did Juma say this morning?" and draft replies from it. Nothing is sent without the user's tap.

8.2 Chat-style learning PARTIAL (chat_affinity_extractor.dart)

  • Learns per-contact tone (formality, emoji use, language mix, typical length) from Oneova chats only, unless the user opts in further.
  • The style profile is stored as features, not raw messages, and is used for suggested replies that the user edits or sends.

8.3 Voice clone TO BUILD

  • Enrollment: the user records a consent sentence plus about 1 minute of samples. The voice model belongs to that account only and can be deleted at any time.
  • Generation runs through Vertex AI on the server, the same setup as the existing Gemini functions with no client API key. It can translate and speak in other languages.
  • Labeling: every AI-voiced message carries a visible "AI voice" badge and an inaudible watermark. Recipients can always tell. The goal is to sound like the user, not to deceive the listener. Undisclosed cloning would breach Play's deceptive-behaviour policy and laws in several markets.
  • Never usable in calls without the user present, and never for anyone else's voice.
